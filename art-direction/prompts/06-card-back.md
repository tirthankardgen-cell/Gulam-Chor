# 06 · One back for every secret

**Save:** `public/art/generated/card-back.webp`

**Canvas:** 768 × 1152, exact 2:3 portrait. Opaque, edge-to-edge.

**Use:** All hidden cards, including the removed Jack.

## Generation prompt

Design the full back of an original Indian family playing card in a warm hand-block-printed style. Exact 2:3 portrait rectangle, flat orthographic view. A narrow aged cream paper edge surrounds a worn terracotta border, followed by a fine muted brass line and a deep cotton teal inner field. Fill the field with small repeated buta/leaf or diamond motifs, lightly uneven like a carved block impression on old card stock. At the center place one restrained symmetrical ornamental medallion containing a simple spade-and-leaf emblem, graceful and slightly mischievous. The back should look like a treasured household deck, not a casino product.

Make the entire arrangement rotationally symmetric at 180 degrees. Use only warm cream #F5E6C5, deep teal #2B4A42, terracotta #AF6443, and muted brass #DFB36D. Aged matte paper with subtle gouache texture; fine irregularities, but strong clean recognition when reduced to 60×90px. Include sufficient cream edge to separate adjacent overlapping cards. No cast shadow outside the card, and no rendered 3D thickness. The website applies its own rounded clipping and shadow.

Absolutely no readable rank, number, face, hidden marker, suit-dependent detail, text, title, logo wordmark, scratch specific to one card, or asymmetry that signals card identity. This single identical file is used for every hidden card. No glitter, glossy plastic, metallic foil, neon, poker chips, photorealism, drop shadow baked into the background, or watermark.

## Review before saving

Inspect at 60×90px and rotate it 180°. It must look the same either way. Ensure no face or text peeks through. Do not produce separate back variants for different ranks or suits; that would undermine blind drawing.
